## Important Installation Lesson — Axle Seating
Splines engaged does not necessarily mean the CV axle is fully seated and locked into the transaxle.

During reinstallation, the axle splines aligned correctly and the axle slid into the transaxle, but stopped before the retaining ring fully locked. A driving wedge was carefully positioned so the CV boot would not be damaged, and controlled taps were used to provide the final seating force. The axle then moved fully into position and locked in place.

### Future procedure
- Confirm splines are aligned before applying seating force.
- Keep the axle straight to avoid damaging the new seal lip.
- Protect the CV boot at all times.
- If the axle slides in but stops short, do not assume it is locked merely because the splines have engaged.
- Use controlled seating force only after alignment is confirmed.
- Verify final axle position and retaining-ring engagement before reassembly and road testing.

## Quality-Control Note
Torque specifications for future vehicle work should be vehicle-specific and component-specific and verified before final tightening. Do not rely on an approximate torque value when an exact specification is available.
